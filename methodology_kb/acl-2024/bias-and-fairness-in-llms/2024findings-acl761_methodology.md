# Understanding the Impacts of Language Technologies’ Performance Disparities on African American Language Speakers

**Source**: https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.761/

## [NEUTRAL] Quantitative methods for performance disparity measurement
Using quantitative approaches (e.g., word error rates, bias metrics) to identify and measure performance gaps between AAL speakers and WME speakers in language technologies

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: When evaluating fairness-related harms in language technologies; useful for measuring extent of disparities but insufficient for understanding human impacts

**Evidence**: "Although quantitative methods can uncover the existence and extent of performance disparities, they do not help us understand the impacts of these performance disparities."

## [POSITIVE] Qualitative semi-structured interviews
Conducting in-depth semi-structured interviews with 19 AAL speakers to understand their lived experiences with language technologies

**Delta**: revealed two major themes: invisible labor and perceived exclusion from design processes
**Condition**: When seeking to understand human impacts of performance disparities on speakers of underrepresented languages and language varieties

**Evidence**: "qualitative methods can be used to probe deeper into issues around social inequalities and illuminate the relationships between linguistic discrimination and failures of language technologies"

## [POSITIVE] Inductive thematic analysis
A qualitative data analysis method where researchers create codes to label relevant passages and inductively group them into higher-level themes through iterative discussions and consensus-building

**Delta**: identified two primary themes from interview data
**Condition**: Applied to interview transcripts to understand AAL speakers' experiences with language technologies

**Evidence**: "we conducted an inductive thematic analysis, following Braun and Clarke (2006)... inductively grouping these codes into higher-level themes through iterative discussions and consensus-building exercises with multiple members of the research team"

## [NEGATIVE] ASR training on non-diverse datasets
Training automated speech recognition systems predominantly on White Mainstream English data without sufficient inclusion of AAL speech data

**Delta**: higher word error rates for African American users than for white users
**Condition**: Affects AAL speakers using ASR technologies such as Alexa, Siri, and Google Assistant

**Evidence**: "If you're using the same standard English database to teach different types of language technology, then the same inherent biases against AAL will be put into it."

## [NEGATIVE] Proactive code-switching to WME by users
AAL speakers adapting their speech patterns to sound more like White Mainstream English in order to be understood by language technologies

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: Undertaken by AAL speakers as invisible labor when using ASR technologies; experienced as effortful and identity-compromising

**Evidence**: "you have to alter your voice to make it more white sounding... I feel like I got to change me to interface with this technology and that's just not worth the effort."

## [NEGATIVE] Manual output correction by users
AAL speakers manually correcting errors in language technology outputs (e.g., transcriptions) due to poor recognition of AAL

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: Occurs when language technologies fail to correctly recognize AAL speech, particularly in transcription services

**Evidence**: "I often have to listen and manually correct these transcripts, causing my research to take more time... if I have to change the sentence three or four times, I might as well just do it manually."

## [NEGATIVE] Manual text input instead of ASR
Users bypassing ASR technologies entirely and typing inputs manually due to repeated recognition failures

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: Adopted by AAL speakers as a workaround when ASR technologies consistently fail to recognize their speech

**Evidence**: "Look, I could type it faster than trying to speech-to-text. It makes me not want to use the technology, because it doesn't work for me and how I communicate."

## [NEGATIVE] Delegating device interaction to others
AAL speakers asking other people (e.g., family members who speak WME) to interact with smart home devices on their behalf

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: Occurs when code-switching to WME is too difficult or still ineffective for AAL speakers using smart home devices

**Evidence**: "I got a Google Home. Ah, man, I don't even talk to it. I let my daughter or somebody talk to it because it never understands."

## [NEGATIVE] Exclusion of AAL speakers from design processes
Language technology development teams lacking meaningful inclusion of AAL speakers, leading to gaps in knowledge about AAL and resulting in performance disparities

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: Systemic issue in language technology development that contributes to poor performance for AAL speakers

**Evidence**: "I don't ever blame the technology; I blame the people that made it... clearly there aren't Black coders in the room to be like, 'Hey, you know what, this isn't picking up on these key words for Black speakers.'"

## [POSITIVE] Contextualized human-centered evaluation
Evaluation approach that combines qualitative and novel quantitative methods (e.g., measuring correction frequency, code-switching rates) to understand the human impacts of performance disparities

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: Proposed as a complement to existing quantitative disparity measurement for underrepresented language variety speakers

**Evidence**: "we urge NLP researchers and practitioners to invest in developing contextualized and human-centered evaluations of language technologies that seek to understand the impacts of performance disparities on speakers of underrepresented languages and language varieties"

## [NEGATIVE] Auto-correct and grammar tools applying WME norms
Text augmentation tools like Grammarly and auto-correct flagging AAL usage as errors, reinforcing linguistic discrimination

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: Affects AAL speakers using writing assistance tools in personal contexts; viewed positively in professional/formal writing contexts by some users

**Evidence**: "Why is it assumed that when I type AAL, it's a typo? I meant what I said."

## [NEUTRAL] Context-dependent acceptance of WME correction tools
Some AAL speakers selectively accepting grammar and word-choice corrections from language tools in professional contexts while rejecting them in personal contexts

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: Applies when AAL speakers navigate professional versus personal communication contexts

**Evidence**: "I don't have auto-correct activated for my text messages, but when writing e-mails or using Microsoft Word, as it corrects grammar and gives your alternatives for word use... I love it for that!"

## [NEUTRAL] Snowball sampling for participant recruitment
Recruiting interview participants through a combination of internal message boards, social media posts, and word-of-mouth referrals to reach AAL-speaking participants

**Delta**: 19 participants across 11 U.S. states covering all five major geographical regions
**Condition**: Used for participant recruitment; may limit generalizability to full range of African American diaspora

**Evidence**: "We recruited participants through posts on a large technology company's internal message boards for affinity groups, posts on social media, and snowball sampling through email and word of mouth."
