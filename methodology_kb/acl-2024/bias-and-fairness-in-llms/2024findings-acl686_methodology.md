# Identifying and Mitigating Annotation Bias in Natural Language Understanding using Causal Mediation Analysis

**Source**: https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.686/

## [POSITIVE] Causal Mediation Analysis (CMA) for NLU
A framework that quantifies how much each model component (neuron) mediates annotation biases in NLU tasks by computing Natural Indirect Effects (NIE) using counterfactual interventions at the representation level.

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Applied to BERT-base-uncased on NLI, fact verification, and paraphrase identification tasks

**Evidence**: "Experimental results show that we can reduce the impact of annotation artifacts through two bias mitigation methods. Results from the ablation study confirm that CMA is effective in identifying neurons that need to be rectified."

## [POSITIVE] Representation-level Counterfactual Generation
Instead of automatic text editing, counterfactuals are generated by aggregating activation outcomes from bias-leaning samples and using a set-bias operation to replace neuron activations, enabling CMA without text modification.

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Applied across NLI, fact verification, and paraphrase identification tasks

**Evidence**: "In contrast to previous research (Vig et al., 2020; Finlayson et al., 2021), our method does not rely on automatic text editing to create a counterfactual scenario for NLU tasks. This allows us to cover a wider range of NLU tasks, e.g., NLI, fact verification, and paraphrase identification."

## [POSITIVE] Causal-Grounded Masking
Post-hoc bias mitigation method that masks out the top neurons (top 700 by NIE score) with the highest mediated bias effect by multiplying their activations by zero, then scaling remaining neurons by 1/p.

**Delta**: +4.70 accuracy on HANS (baseline: 59.35 → 64.05); +8.77 on HANS over PoE (63.45 → 68.12); +7.07 MaF1 on PAWS over baseline (31.93 → 39.08)
**Condition**: Applied post-hoc to BERT-base-uncased and debiased variants on MNLI/HANS and QQP/PAWS; smaller improvements on FEVER

**Evidence**: "Table 1 shows that when we apply bias mitigation methods based on the neurons obtained from the CMA process, we can significantly improve the performances on the challenge sets compared to the baseline on MNLI and QQP."

## [POSITIVE] Gradient Unlearning
Post-hoc bias mitigation that selectively reverses gradient updates on weight blocks tied to the top-700 highest-NIE neurons, trained only on 'advantaged samples' (correctly predicted by bias model but incorrectly by main model).

**Delta**: +3.00 accuracy on HANS (baseline: 59.35 → 62.35); +3.26 on HANS over Debiasing Masks (66.79 → 70.05); +11.38 MaF1 on PAWS over Debiasing Masks (43.04 → 54.42)
**Condition**: Applied post-hoc to BERT-base-uncased and debiased variants; best combined with Debiasing Masks on PAWS

**Evidence**: "Table 1 shows that when we apply bias mitigation methods based on the neurons obtained from the CMA process, we can significantly improve the performances on the challenge sets compared to the baseline on MNLI and QQP."

## [POSITIVE] CMA-guided Neuron Selection (vs. Random)
Using NIE scores from CMA to select which neurons to manipulate, rather than selecting neurons randomly.

**Delta**: +4.78 accuracy on HANS for masking (59.27 random → 64.05 CMA); +2.49 for gradient unlearning (59.86 random → 62.35 CMA)
**Condition**: Ablation study on baseline BERT model evaluated on MNLI/HANS

**Evidence**: "If random neurons are used (w/o CMA), the HANS dataset results drop from 64.05 to 59.27 for causal-grounded masking, and from 62.35 to 59.86 for gradient unlearning. This suggests that neurons selected by the CMA process are components that mediate more bias."

## [POSITIVE] Advantaged Sample Selection for Gradient Unlearning
Selecting training samples where the bias model predicts correctly but the main model predicts incorrectly ('advantaged samples') to focus gradient unlearning on the most bias-affected examples.

**Delta**: +3.01 accuracy on HANS (59.34 random → 62.35 advantaged)
**Condition**: Ablation study on baseline BERT model evaluated on MNLI/HANS

**Evidence**: "The performance on the HANS dataset drops from 62.35 to 59.34 when advantaged samples are selected randomly. This highlights the importance of identifying biased samples."

## [POSITIVE] Focusing Bias Mitigation on Final Transformer Layers
CMA reveals that annotation bias effects are concentrated in the last few layers (especially layers 10 and 11), so mitigation efforts are directed there.

**Delta**: Layer 11 intervention has much larger impact than layer 10; NIE saturates at ~10,000 neurons
**Condition**: Observed across MNLI, FEVER, and QQP datasets on BERT-base-uncased

**Evidence**: "The CMA results suggest that the top 5% of neurons in the last few layers have the highest effect, especially layers 10 and 11... The results show that intervening on the layer with the highest NIE can also have a much larger impact."

## [POSITIVE] Applying CMA Mitigation on Top of Existing Debiasing Methods
Stacking causal-grounded masking or gradient unlearning on top of already-debiased models (reweighting, PoE, debiasing masks) for additional robustness gains.

**Delta**: PoE + Masking: 68.12 HANS vs PoE alone 63.45; Debiasing Masks + Gradient Unlearning: 70.05 HANS vs Debiasing Masks alone 66.79; Reweighting + Masking: 51.05 PAWS vs Reweighting alone 42.08
**Condition**: Applied to reweighting, PoE, and debiasing masks models across NLI and paraphrase identification tasks

**Evidence**: "Furthermore, both bias mitigation methods can be used on models trained using competitive methods for more improvement. This suggests we can modify their behavior by identifying the biased neurons to reduce the sensitivity against known spurious correlation features."

## [POSITIVE] Post-hoc Application Without Retraining
Both bias mitigation methods are applied post-hoc to existing fine-tuned models without training from scratch or adding additional modules.

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: General design choice applicable to any fine-tuned BERT-based NLU model

**Evidence**: "The bias mitigation methods can be applied post-hoc to any existing models without training from scratch or adding an additional module. In this way, users can import our set of weights and use the exact same code for their inference module without requiring an additional engineering effort."

## [NEUTRAL] Existing Debiasing Methods (Reweighting, PoE, Debiasing Masks) Alone
Standard fine-tuning-time debiasing techniques including loss reweighting, product-of-experts ensembling, and debiasing masks applied without CMA-guided post-hoc mitigation.

**Delta**: Bias still concentrated in specific components; NIE patterns similar to baseline
**Condition**: Applied to BERT-base-uncased on MNLI, FEVER, QQP; evaluated via CMA structural analysis

**Evidence**: "NIEs of these methods also have a similar pattern to the baseline's NIEs... this analysis suggests that model components still mediate annotation biases despite using the debiased fine-tuning methods."

## [NEGATIVE] Masking Too Many Neurons (>2500)
Selecting an excessively large number of neurons (2500+) to mask during causal-grounded masking.

**Delta**: dev-matched drops from 80.21 (700 neurons) to 68.34 (2500 neurons) to 53.69 (5000 neurons); HANS also degrades at extreme values
**Condition**: Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis for causal-grounded masking on MNLI

**Evidence**: "When the number of neurons is large (from 2,500 onwards), the masking method fails to gain any improvement and also fails to maintain the in-distribution scores."

## [NEGATIVE] Extreme Neuron Count and Learning Rate for Gradient Unlearning
Using very large numbers of neurons (2500+) combined with high learning rate amplification (10x) in gradient unlearning.

**Delta**: dev-matched drops to 62.12 (2500 neurons, scale 10); HANS also degrades to 59.28
**Condition**: Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis for gradient unlearning on MNLI

**Evidence**: "The gradient unlearning method also fails when the number of neurons and the learning rate amplification are extreme."

## [NEUTRAL] CLS Token Representation for CMA
Exclusively using the [CLS] token position activations for causal mediation analysis in NLU classification tasks.

**Delta**: Not separately quantified
**Condition**: Design choice for CMA implementation across all three NLU tasks

**Evidence**: "As NLU tasks inherently involve classification, we exclusively focus on the activations of the transformer model at the [CLS] token position."

## [POSITIVE] Dropout-style Activation Scaling After Masking
After masking top-NIE neurons, the remaining neurons' activations are scaled up by 1/p (inverse proportion of unmasked neurons), similar to dropout scaling.

**Delta**: Not separately quantified
**Condition**: Applied within causal-grounded masking method

**Evidence**: "Similar to Dropout (Srivastava et al., 2014), we scale up the activation outcomes of the remaining neurons by 1/p, which is the inverse proportion of the unmasked neurons."

## [NEUTRAL] Bias Mitigation on FEVER Task
Applying CMA-based bias mitigation methods to the fact verification (FEVER) task.

**Delta**: Small or no statistically significant improvements on FEVER Symmetric challenge sets
**Condition**: Applied to FEVER dataset; conjecture is that bias samples are harder to identify for counterfactual generation

**Evidence**: "The robustness improvements for NLI and paraphrase identification tasks are more substantial than the fact verification task. When we apply the causal-grounded masking method, small improvements can only be observed on both FEVER Symmetric challenge sets for the PoE model and on the Fever Symmetric Version 1 for the debiasing masks method."
