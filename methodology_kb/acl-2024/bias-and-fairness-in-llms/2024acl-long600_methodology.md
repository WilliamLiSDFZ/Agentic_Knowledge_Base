# Measuring Political Bias in Large Language Models: What Is Said and How It Is Said

**Source**: https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.600/

## [POSITIVE] Two-tiered Political Bias Framework
A framework that evaluates LLM political bias at two levels: (1) political stance analysis and (2) framing bias analysis decomposed into content and style

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Measuring political bias in LLMs across diverse political topics

**Evidence**: "This study introduces an interpretable and granular framework for measuring political bias in LLM-generated content, going beyond traditional political-orientation level analyses."

## [POSITIVE] Extreme Anchor Comparison for Stance Analysis
Quantifying stance by measuring semantic similarity between model outputs and two opposed reference distributions (proponent and opponent) generated by the same LLM

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Political stance estimation across 10 political topics

**Evidence**: "we implement a method of extreme anchor comparison, quantifying the similarity between model outputs and two opposed stances – advocacy and opposition – across various political subjects"

## [POSITIVE] SentenceBERT for Distance Measurement
Using SBERT cosine similarity with nearest-neighbor retrieval to measure distributional distance between model generations and anchor reference distributions

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Semantic similarity measurement for stance estimation

**Evidence**: "We implement distance measurement d{pro/opp} = d(P(Ŷ)||P(Y{pro/opp})) using sentence embedding from SentenceBERT (SBERT) (Reimers and Gurevych, 2019). We employ SBERT to analyze semantic similarities at a conceptual level rather than at the word level, which is a common practice in semantic analysis"

## [POSITIVE] News Headline Generation as Probe
Prompting LLMs to generate news headlines about political topics as the evaluation task, rather than direct opinion questions or survey-style prompts

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Eliciting politically biased content from LLMs for evaluation

**Evidence**: "we probe models by conducting news headline generation about political topics. This choice of scope is grounded in the insight that news article titles, as demonstrated by (Lee et al., 2022), serve as effective indicators of framing bias."

## [POSITIVE] Boydstun Frame Dimensions for Content Bias
Using 15 cross-cutting topic-agnostic framing dimensions (e.g., economics, morality, health and safety) from Boydstun et al. (2014) to classify what content LLMs emphasize

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Content bias analysis across political topics

**Evidence**: "We adopt the 15 cross-cutting framing dimensions, such as 'economics', 'morality', 'Health and safety', 'cultural identity', developed by Boydstun et al. (2014) based on framing literature for analyzing political policy. These frame dimensions are general enough to be applied to various political topics."

## [POSITIVE] NER-based Entity Frame Extraction
Using a pre-trained Named Entity Recognition model to extract frequently mentioned entities and their frequencies from LLM generations as a topic-specific framing signal

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Topic-specific content bias analysis

**Evidence**: "While Boydstun frame dimensions provide an overall understanding of informational framing, analysis over entities gives topic-specific granular information such as frequently mentioned political figures, countries, or topic-specific parties/organizations. The frequent mention of certain entities can be interpreted as one sort of framing technique."

## [POSITIVE] Target Sentiment Analysis for Style Bias
Using a target sentiment analysis model to estimate lexical polarity (positive, negative, neutral) towards specific named entities in LLM-generated content

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Style bias analysis for political framing

**Evidence**: "The inverse function h^{-1}_S : P(Ŷ) → S is implemented with a target sentiment analysis model. To capture how the content is said, it analyzes the lexical polarity (positive, negative, or neutral) towards the target entity in a generation."

## [POSITIVE] GPT-3.5-instruct Zero-shot Frame Classification
Using GPT-3.5-instruct via prompting for zero-shot classification of headlines into Boydstun's 15 frame dimensions

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Frame dimension classification for content bias analysis

**Evidence**: "Recognizing the zero-shot classification ability of ChatGPT, we utilize the instruction-tuned model (GPT3.5-instruct) by prompting."

## [NEUTRAL] LLM-generated Reference Anchors
Generating proponent and opponent reference distributions using the same LLM under evaluation with stance-specific prompts, rather than using external human-written references

**Delta**: trade-off noted
**Condition**: Reference anchor generation for stance analysis; may not satisfy equal-norm assumption

**Evidence**: "The rationale behind using 'generated' pro-/anti sentences as anchor references was to reflect the 'content bias' of models... Yet, there exists the trade-off between satisfying the equal-norm assumption and being effective in capturing the response distribution of each LLM"

## [NEGATIVE] Larger Model Size
Using larger parameter count LLMs (e.g., Yi-34B vs Yi-6B, Falcon-40B vs Falcon-7B) with the expectation of reduced political bias

**Delta**: Yi-6B: 4 neutral stances out of 10 topics, media bias rate 1.9%; Yi-34B: media bias rate 3.8%
**Condition**: Political neutrality across Yi and Falcon model families

**Evidence**: "the Yi-6B model, despite its smaller size, exhibits a lower overall stance (4 neutral stances out of 10 topics) and media bias rates (1.9%) compared to its larger counterpart, Yi-34B (3.8%)... These observations indicate that increased model capacity does not inherently ensure reduced political bias"

## [NEUTRAL] Multilingual Pre-training
Training LLMs on multilingual data (e.g., Arabic-English for Jais, Chinese-English for Yi) affecting the geographic and cultural focus of generated political content

**Delta**: Jais features UAE-related topics in 64% of examined areas; Yi shows less US-centric content
**Condition**: Content focus and stance intensity in multilingual vs. English-only models

**Evidence**: "the Arabic-English bi-lingual model like JAIS, which prominently features UAE-related topics in 64% of examined areas, unlike other models. Despite this geographical preference in content, JAIS exhibits lower stance intensity, as indicated by their ∥⃗s∥ norms."

## [POSITIVE] Topic-specific Fine-grained Analysis
Evaluating political bias at the individual topic level rather than aggregating into a single left-right political orientation score

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Measuring political bias across diverse political topics

**Evidence**: "LLMs show different political views depending on the topic, such as being more liberal on reproductive rights and more conservative on immigration... This highlights the importance of nuanced, issue-specific analysis to understand the complex biases in these models."

## [POSITIVE] Statistical Significance Filtering for Stance
Using a p-value threshold of 0.01 to determine whether a model's stance difference is significant enough to label as proponent or opponent, otherwise labeling as neutral

**Delta**: only 10.9% of cases labeled neutral (12 out of 110 combinations)
**Condition**: Stance label assignment in political stance analysis

**Evidence**: "If there is no significant difference from other similarity scores with a p-value of 0.01, then the predicted stance label is neutral."

## [POSITIVE] Cross-model Comparative Framing Analysis
Comparing content and style variables across multiple LLMs rather than against a single golden reference, to identify relative biases

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Framing bias analysis when no ground-truth neutral reference exists

**Evidence**: "rather than measuring the deviation of C and S compared to some golden references, we compare different C and S across a diverse range of models. This granular methodology for framing bias analysis facilitates a deep dive into the LLM-generated content"

## [NEGATIVE] Binary Proponent/Opponent Anchor Design
Representing political stance using only two extreme anchor points (proponent and opponent) for each topic

**Delta**: descriptive limitation noted
**Condition**: Topics with more than two distinct political positions

**Evidence**: "The evaluation of the LLM stance on specific topics was conducted using two anchor points – proponent and opponent – in this paper. This approach, while practical, may not fully represent topics that encompass a broader spectrum of perspectives, indicating a simplification that could overlook multifaceted stances."
